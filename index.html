What The Ariel is and why it exists

What it is

A daily feed of books, long-form journalism and true stories matched to your development interests - with rights intelligence attached, so you can move fast on what matters.

Bespoke from day one. Sharpens every time your team saves, rejects or comments. Add the Substacks, trade publications and niche outlets you already trust - they go into your source list alongside the standard ones.

Why it exists

The volume of books, articles and pre-publication material crossing the screen industry every month is unreadable by any one person. Scouts, agents and editorial taste do the deciding well. The systematic surfacing - which feeds that judgement, not replaces it - has been the gap.

The Ariel is built for that gap: personalised story intelligence that gives producers and development teams more of the right material to choose from, every day.

How it reaches you

A daily email, a living dashboard

Each morning, a curated email digest lands in your inbox with the day’s most relevant stories. Every email links straight to your personalised dashboard - a private web app where you can dig deeper, take action, and train your feed.

The morning email

A concise summary of new stories matched to your brief, designed to scan in under two minutes. Styled to your dashboard’s look and feel - not a generic newsletter. Click any story to open it directly in your dashboard.

Your personalised dashboard

A private web app with your full feed, expanded pitch notes, rights intelligence and “Why Now” context. Use Research to flag stories for your development team, More like this to sharpen your feed, or Archive to clear the noise. Every action teaches your dashboard what you want to see next.

Your dashboard, your identity

Every client gets a bespoke dashboard

No two dashboards look the same. We design each one around your brand, your genre interests, and the way your team works. Underneath, they all share the same engine: recommendations scored against your slate, one-click research packs, and starred submissions sent in by literary agents with rights available.
